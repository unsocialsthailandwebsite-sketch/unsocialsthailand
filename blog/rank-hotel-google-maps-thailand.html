How to Rank Your Hotel on Google Maps in Thailand

Published August 2026 · Unsocials Thailand

Ask any hotel owner in Thailand where their direct bookings come from and they will talk about Instagram, or their website, or the OTAs they resent. Almost nobody says Google Maps. Which is odd, because when a traveller lands at Suvarnabhumi and types "hotel near me" into their phone, the map pack is the entire decision.

Three results. That is it. Everything below the fold may as well not exist.

How Google actually ranks the map pack

Three inputs: relevance, distance and prominence. You cannot move your hotel, so distance is fixed. Relevance and prominence are entirely in your control, and your Google Business Profile carries roughly 32% of total local ranking weight on its own.

That single number should reframe how you think about this. No other asset you own moves local visibility that much for that little money.

Finish the profile. All of it.

Completeness is now a direct ranking factor. Profiles at 100% information completeness see up to a 50% visibility boost over partially completed competitors in the same area.

Most Thai hotel profiles sit at perhaps 60%. Missing amenity attributes, no check-in and check-out times, no accessibility fields, no description, half the photo categories empty. Every blank field is a competitor moving above you for free.

Your primary category is the highest-leverage field on the page

Primary category carries more weight than almost anything else in local search. It decides whether you appear in the map pack at all for a given query.

Get specific. "Resort Hotel", "Boutique Hotel", "Extended Stay Hotel" and "Serviced Apartment" are different categories that surface for different searches. Then stack secondary categories for what you actually offer. A hotel with a genuine rooftop bar should carry the bar category. A property with a spa should carry the spa category. Each one opens a query you were previously invisible for.

Photos are now read, not just looked at

This is the change most operators have not caught up with. Google AI now analyses the content of the images you upload. If someone searches "hotel with rooftop pool Pattaya" and your profile carries well-tagged rooftop pool photos, you are more likely to surface for it.

Which means your photo library is no longer decoration. It is a keyword surface. Sea view, infinity pool, family room, co-working space, breakfast, beach access, pet friendly. Shoot the things people search for, and upload them consistently rather than in one batch two years ago.

Reviews: velocity beats volume, and LINE is the Thai unlock

Everyone knows reviews matter. What operators miss is that Google reads velocity more than it reads totals. Two hundred reviews with nothing new in eight months reads as a business losing relevance. Fifteen reviews a month, every month, reads as a business people are actively choosing.

Here is the part specific to Thailand, and it is the highest-return tactic in this article: the most effective review generation channel for Thai businesses is LINE Official Account. A review request sent over LINE OA immediately after a positive interaction converts at significantly higher rates than email or SMS.

Your guests are already on LINE. They are not opening a post-stay email. Build the request into your LINE flow at checkout, while the stay is still good in their memory, and review velocity stops being something you hope for.

The bilingual problem almost nobody solves

A hotel in Pattaya or Phuket is discovered by at least four different audiences: Thai residents, expats, international tourists and B2B buyers. They search in different languages, use different location modifiers and carry different intent.

Most properties optimise for exactly one of them, in English, and quietly forfeit the rest. Thai-language discovery in particular is far less contested than the English equivalent, and it is where domestic weekend demand lives.

In Pattaya, English alone is not the ceiling either. The same nationality shift driving the property market is filling hotel rooms, which we covered in targeting international buyers. Russian and Chinese language coverage is a genuine competitive gap, not a nice-to-have.

NAP consistency, and the Thai address problem

Name, address and phone number must match everywhere Google finds them. Mismatched data between your profile, your website and every directory you were listed on years ago confuses the algorithm and costs you ranking.

In Thailand this breaks more often than elsewhere, because addresses get transliterated inconsistently. Soi versus Road versus the Thai spelling, moo numbers included or dropped, province spelled three different ways. Pick one canonical English form and one Thai form, then enforce them everywhere without exception.

Post weekly

Profiles posting weekly consistently outperform inactive ones in local rankings. Offers, events, seasonal packages, a new restaurant opening. It takes ten minutes and most of your competitors have never posted once.

A 30-day plan

  1. Audit your profile to 100% completeness. Every field, every attribute, no exceptions.
  2. Re-examine your primary category, then add every legitimate secondary one.
  3. Shoot and upload photos of the specific things guests search for, tagged properly.
  4. Build a review request into your LINE checkout flow. This is the single highest-return item here.
  5. Fix NAP across your site and every directory carrying an old listing.
  6. Add Thai-language coverage, then Russian or Chinese if your market warrants it.
  7. Post weekly. Put it in someone's calendar.

None of this requires ad budget. It is the cheapest distribution available to a hotel in Thailand, and it compounds. Pair it with the direct booking work in reducing OTA dependence and you are taking guests off commission at the same time.
